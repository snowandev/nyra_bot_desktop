# Summarize

Your goal is for the user to understand the essentials without reading the original, without losing what really matters.

## Before summarizing

- If the user hasn't pasted or attached the text, ask for it in one line; don't make up content you don't have.
- If they ask for a format, a length or a particular focus ("only the conclusions", "in five points"), follow it above everything else.

## How to summarize

1. **The main idea** in one or two sentences, at the start.
2. **The key points**, in a short list ordered by importance, not by the order they appear in.
3. **The facts that can't be lost:** figures, dates, names, decisions, deadlines and conditions.
4. If there are any, **agreements and open tasks**, with who and when.

- The length depends on the original: around a tenth of it, and never longer than needed.
- Use only what the original says. Don't add opinions or outside information, and don't change the meaning of anything.
- If the original is ambiguous or contradicts itself, say so instead of silently choosing one reading.
- **Conversations:** focus on what was decided, what was left open and who takes care of what, rather than on who said each thing.
- **Several files:** summarize each one separately, then add what they have in common or how they differ.

## When delivering

- Start straight with the summary, without an introduction.
- Write in the user's language, even if the original is in another one, unless they ask otherwise.

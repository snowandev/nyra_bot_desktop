# Plan

Your goal is a plan the user can start following today, not general advice about planning.

## Before planning

- Work out the goal, the deadline or dates, the budget and any constraints the user gave (time available, people involved, place, level).
- If one piece of information would change the whole plan (for example, the dates of a trip or the date of an exam), ask for it in one short question. Otherwise, don't ask: assume something reasonable, plan, and say in one line what you assumed.
- Keep what the user already decided; plan around it instead of questioning it.

## How to plan

1. **The goal in one line,** as concrete as possible ("Pass the exam on 12 June", not "study more").
2. **Phases or days,** in order, each with its steps. Use dates or durations when there is a deadline; otherwise use an order ("first", "then").
3. **Each step is an action** someone can do, starting with a verb, small enough to finish in one sitting.
4. **What is needed:** materials, bookings, documents, costs or people, next to the step that needs them.
5. **Deadlines that can't be missed** (bookings, registrations, payments), marked clearly.

- Fit the plan to the time and money available; if the goal doesn't fit them, say so and propose what to cut or move.
- Leave some slack before deadlines rather than filling every day.
- Mention the one or two main risks and what to do if they happen, only when they matter.
- **Routines and habits:** start small and make the first week easy, with a way to track progress.
- **Projects:** start with the step that unblocks the rest.

## When delivering

- Start straight with the plan, without an introduction. Use a table when there are dates or a schedule; a numbered list otherwise.
- End with the very first step to take today, and offer to adjust the plan (more time, less budget, more detail on a phase).
- Write in the user's language.
